A short, evidence-based guide

PCOS, explained clearly.

Polycystic ovary syndrome can affect people in different ways. This page offers general background to help you understand the condition and prepare questions for a healthcare professional.

Sources checked 9 November 2026 · WHO and the 2023 international evidence-based guideline

01 · THE BASICS

What is PCOS?

PCOS is a hormonal condition that can affect menstrual cycles and ovulation. It is also associated with changes in androgen hormones and metabolic health. Its causes are not fully understood.

02 · EXPERIENCES VARY

Signs can look different

Some people notice irregular or infrequent periods, acne or oily skin, excess facial or body hair, or scalp hair thinning. Symptoms can differ and may change over time. These signs can also have other causes.

03 · PROFESSIONAL ASSESSMENT

How is it assessed?

There is no single symptom or app result that confirms PCOS. A healthcare professional considers a person’s history and symptoms, may use blood tests or imaging, and checks for other possible causes. Age and life stage matter.

04 · ABOUT ULTRASOUND

An image alone cannot diagnose PCOS

Ultrasound can be one part of an adult assessment, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Some people with PCOS do not have polycystic-appearing ovaries. The international guideline uses different assessment considerations for adolescents.

Keeping a useful record

Dates and notes you choose to record can help you describe what you have noticed during a healthcare conversation. PCOS Insight only organizes those entries; its charts and summaries do not interpret your health.

If changes in your periods, skin or hair are concerning to you, consider discussing them with a qualified healthcare professional. You can bring a copy of your records if that would be helpful.
